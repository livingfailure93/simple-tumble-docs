The demo scene

Open Assets/SimpleTumble/Scenes/SimpleTumbleDemo.unity and press Play. The scene holds a camera, a sun, and one object called Simple Tumble Demo with the TumbleDemo component and the four level packs assigned. Everything else, from the tiles and the cube to the menus, is built in code when the scene starts, so there are no prefabs, models, textures or audio files to look after.

The demo reads the mouse, touch and keyboard through the Input System package. If nothing happens when you tap or press keys, set Project Settings > Player > Active Input Handling to Input System Package or Both.

One component: TumbleDemo

Add TumbleDemo to any object in a scene with a camera and press Play. When it starts, it builds:

  • a Board child with TumbleBoardView, which draws the board, the cube and every gimmick, and TumbleGame, which plays them;
  • the HUD (TumbleHud) and the hub (TumbleHub), both in uGUI with Unity's built-in font;
  • TumbleBeeper for the sounds and TumbleEffects for the effects.

It also gives the camera a plain background colour and a 30 degree field of view, and adds a sun if the scene has no light.

FieldWhat it does
CameraThe camera that shows the board. Empty uses the main camera, and if there is none, one is made.
UiThroughCameraDraw the HUD and the hub through the camera instead of over the screen, so the camera's own picture includes them.
PitchHow steeply the camera looks down at the board to start with, in degrees (55 by default). Players can tilt it while they play.
HdrpLookHDRP only: give the demo the same look as on the other pipelines. Turn it off to use your own volumes and lights.
ClassicPack, StampPack, LocksPack, TrickyTilesPackThe level pack for each mode. Any can be empty, and that mode's Level Pack tab then says how to fill it.

The flow

1

Welcome

The first screen says what Simple Tumble is and where to make your own puzzles, with the publisher's contact at the bottom. Let's play goes on, and About on the mode screen brings it back.

2

Pick a mode

Four cards, each with a picture of a small puzzle of that mode, drawn by the asset's own exporter.

3

Endless or Level Pack

Endless lets you pick a Square board or Shapes (a cross, ring, diamond, circle, heart or L) and a difficulty from 1 to 5 stars, then makes a new puzzle to that order on a background thread. Level Pack shows the mode's levels as numbered buttons: grey still to do, the mode's colour solved, and gold solved on par. Play starts the first level you haven't solved. The demo remembers your choices for each mode while it runs.

4

Play

A card at the top left shows what to do (reach the goal, or stamp the targets) beside a chip of the face that does it, the board and its gimmicks, its stars, your rolls against par and the hints used, how the gimmicks stand (targets stamped, keys taken, gates open, faces pressed, tiles crumbled), and a line for messages such as why a roll was refused. A button bar has Undo, Restart, Hint, Solve and Menu.

5

Win

The cube hops twice, confetti falls, and after a short pause a panel shows your rolls, the par and a rating, with Replay, Menu and Next (or press Enter). In Endless, Next makes a new puzzle to the same order, and in a pack it goes to the next level, then back to the pack after the last one. Menu or Esc returns to the mode cards at any time.

The four modes

ModeEndless puzzles
ClassicReach the goal with the right face down, on a board that grows with the stars, from 5 x 5 at 1 star to 9 x 9 at 5.
StampStamp every target: 2 targets at 1 star, 3 at 2 and 3 stars, 4 at 4 and 5 at 5. From 4 stars every other puzzle wants them in order.
LocksA key or a switch at 1 and 2 stars, both at 3, two switches or both with face locks at 4, and two keys and a switch at 5. Two locks never made a 1 or 2 star puzzle when it was measured.
Tricky TilesA one-way tile (sometimes with an avoid tile) at 1 star, avoid and one-way tiles or a turntable at 2, a turntable with a crumbling tile or a portal at 3, a portal or a turntable with a crumbling and a one-way tile at 4, and a portal with two turntables or a multi-face tile at 5.

Each star level has two recipes, and each new puzzle picks one, so Endless doesn't repeat itself. Shapes boards are two cells bigger than square ones. The recipes are in TumbleEndless, and each one goes through TumblePresets.Tune and verified generation with the stars as its target, so the puzzle you get rates the stars you picked.

The recipes were measured by making 8 puzzles for every mode, board and star level. 312 of the 320 landed on exactly the stars asked, and the slowest took 1.5 seconds. The weakest spot is Locks at 3 stars on Shapes, where 5 of 8 landed on 3 stars and the rest came as close as they could.

The level packs

The demo ships with 44 levels in four packs in Assets/SimpleTumble/Generated. Each was made by the generator from a fixed seed, landed exactly on the stars it was made for, and was played through to par to check it. They are ordinary TumblePuzzlePack assets, so you can open them in the inspector, read their analysis, or export them as printable pictures.

PackLevelsWhat's in it
Classic12Reach the goal, rising from 1 star to 5, with a heart, a ring, a cross and a circle among the boards.
Stamp10Stamp the targets, rising from 1 star to 5, with a diamond, a ring and a heart. Levels 7, 9 and 10 want the targets in order.
Locks10A key, then a switch, then both, then face locks, two switches, and two keys with a switch, rising from 1 star to 5.
Tricky Tiles12One new tile per level to start with (one-way, avoid, turntable, crumbling, portal and multi-face), then mixes of them, rising from 1 star to 5.

Your own packs: make puzzles in the Tumble Generator, choose Save > Pack (all), and drag the pack into a slot on the TumbleDemo component.

Progress: each level's best number of rolls is saved in PlayerPrefs under SimpleTumble.Demo.Best.<pack name>.<level>, so it stays on that device. A level Solve helped with isn't recorded. The win panel says "Level complete!", "New best" or "Your best is" accordingly.

Playing

The controls are listed at the bottom of the screen while you play, and in the Quick Start. A few details:

  • Rolling: the arrow keys, WASD and swipes roll the cube the way that looks closest on screen, so the controls stay right however the camera is turned. A tap beside the cube rolls it toward the tap. A key pressed mid-roll waits for the roll to land.
  • The gimmicks react: a key spins up and away and its doors sink open, switch buttons go down and gates sink or rise, ticks pop up on stamped targets and pressed faces, a crumbling tile drops once the cube rolls off it, the cube shrinks into a portal and grows out of its twin, and a turntable turns with the cube on it.
  • A refused roll rocks the cube toward that way, and the card says why in plain words: "A wall is in the way.", "Door 1 is locked: take key 1 first.", "Gate 2 is shut: press switch 2 to open it." or "The green diamond mustn't touch down on that tile." Faces are named by their colour and shape.
  • Undo plays the last roll backwards, with every gimmick back as it stood. Restart pops the cube back in on its start.
  • Hint floats a blue arrow over the cube the way a best solution rolls first. When there is no way to win from where you are, the card says "There's no way on from here: undo or restart."
  • Solve (or F) plays the rest by itself, one best roll at a time, starting again from the beginning if you have rolled into a dead end. The button says Stop meanwhile, and F or R stops it.
  • The view: the camera frames the board in the space the HUD leaves free, in landscape or portrait, and again whenever the window changes shape. Turn, tilt and zoom it with the keys or the mouse, and when zoomed in it follows the cube.
  • The rating on the win panel is 3 pips on par, 2 a little over (up to 2 rolls, or a quarter of the par on longer puzzles) and 1 beyond that.

Sounds and effects

TumbleBeeper makes every sound in code when the demo starts: a wooden tok as the cube lands (three variants, so a run of rolls doesn't sound robotic), a dull bump for a refused roll, a sparkle for a key, a sliding rumble for a door or gate, a click for a switch, a stamp and a chime for a target, a ping for a face pressed, a whirr for a turntable, a whoosh for a portal, a crackle for a crumbling tile, a hint, a rewind for undo and restart, a win arpeggio and a button blip. M mutes it. There is no music.

TumbleEffects adds small effects made from a pool of tiny cubes: dust where the cube lands or bumps, sparkles over a key, a stamped target or a pressed face, dust round a door or gate as it sinks, a swirl at a portal, chunks from a crumbling tile, and confetti over the whole board for the win. Nothing is created or destroyed while you play, and because the effects use the demo's ordinary materials, they look the same in every render pipeline.

Render pipelines

The demo works in URP (recommended), HDRP and the Built-in pipeline without any changes. Its materials use the active pipeline's default lit shader, and a build step keeps that shader in your builds, so the demo doesn't draw pink on a phone. The faces, numbers and marks are baked into lit textures, so nothing needs an unlit or text shader.

HDRP lights in physical units, sets the exposure on its own and takes its ambient light from a sky, so the demo would come out dark and tinted. With HdrpLook on, DemoHdrp adds a global volume with a fixed exposure, no tone mapping or bloom, and a gradient sky in the demo's colours, clears the camera to the demo's background colour, and sets the sun in lux. That code only compiles when the HDRP package is installed.

The shipped scene was checked in all three pipelines: every material on the pipeline's lit shader, nothing pink, and the pictures within a few shades of each other.

Using the pieces yourself

The demo components work on their own too. The smallest setup is one TumbleGame, which adds its TumbleBoardView automatically:

using SimpleTumble;
using SimpleTumble.Demo;

var game = new GameObject("Tumble").AddComponent<TumbleGame>();
game.Begin(puzzle);                                    // build the board and start a game
game.View.FrameCamera(Camera.main, pitch: 50f);        // the camera is yours: frame the board once
game.Message += text => Debug.Log(text);               // a refused roll, in the player's words
game.Won += g => Debug.Log($"Solved in {g.Moves} rolls, par {g.Par}");
game.Roll(Cube.East);                                  // roll from your own code (keys, swipes and taps work too)
  • Input: TumbleGame reads the keys, swipes and taps itself while it is enabled. Your code can roll too, with Roll(dir), Push(screenDirection) and Tap(screenPoint), and call Undo(), Restart(), ShowHint() and StartAutoSolve(). Set PointerBlocked so a press over your own UI doesn't roll the cube.
  • The camera is yours unless you turn on CameraControls, which lets the game frame the board and gives players the turn, tilt and zoom controls. FrameAreas keeps the board in the parts of the screen your UI leaves free.
  • Events: Moved after every change of position, Won once when it is solved, Message for things a player should read, Cue for every moment worth a sound, and Moment for the same moments with a position and colour for effects.
  • A HUD: add a TumbleHud and set its Game, or read Moves, Par, Solved, HintsUsed and CanUndo into your own UI.
  • The look: the colours and materials are in DemoLook.cs, the meshes in TumbleMeshes.cs and TumbleBoardView.cs, and RollTime sets how long a roll takes (0.24 seconds by default).
  • Without the 3D view: keep a TumblePlay and use TumbleRules directly, as in the Quick Start.
