Assemblies and numbers

AssemblyWhat it holds
SimpleTumbleThe core: the generator, the solver, the play rules, the puzzle data, the pictures and the ScriptableObjects. It references no packages, and everything except the asset types is plain C# that also runs outside Unity.
SimpleTumble.DemoThe 3D demo: the board, the game, the HUD, the hub, sounds and effects. It uses the Input System and Unity UI packages, and HDRP when that package is installed.
SimpleTumble.EditorThe Tumble Generator window, the inspectors, the documentation menu and the build step that keeps the demo's shader. Editor only.

Everything below is in the SimpleTumble namespace unless it says SimpleTumble.Demo. The core uses plain numbers throughout:

  • Cells are numbered y * Width + x, with row 0 at the bottom, so cell 0 is the bottom left corner.
  • Directions are 0 North (y + 1), 1 East (x + 1), 2 South and 3 West. The opposite of dir is (dir + 2) % 4.
  • Faces are 0 to 5, and face f is opposite face 5 - f. The core gives them no look. The asset's own pictures and demo show them as a red dot, a blue square, a yellow triangle, a green diamond, a purple star and an orange hexagon.
  • Orientations are the 24 ways the cube can sit, 0 to 23. Cube turns them into faces and back.

TumbleGenerator

The entry point. Every method copies and clamps the settings first, so your object is never changed, and the same settings with the same seed always give the same puzzle. A null settings object means the defaults, and an empty seed means a fresh random one.

MethodWhat it does
TumbleData Generate(TumbleSettings settings)One puzzle from this exact seed, solved and rated.
TumbleData GenerateVerified(TumbleSettings settings, CancellationToken cancel = default) Tries the seed, then the seed with #1, #2 and so on, up to MaxAttempts, until a puzzle is sound (solvable, inside the par window and the coverage, and with no way to get stuck when NoSoftLocks is on), has every gimmick it asked for, and is rated TargetDifficulty stars (0 means any). On a miss it returns the closest puzzle with a note. If it is cancelled before the first puzzle, it throws OperationCanceledException.
TumbleData GenerateVerified(TumbleSettings settings, CancellationToken cancel, IProgress<TumbleProgress> progress, double timeLimitSeconds = 0) The same with progress and a time limit. The progress hears about 10 times a second which board it is on and what that board is doing. A cancel stops it within a fraction of a second, even in the middle of a board. Past the time limit (0 means none) it returns the closest puzzle of the boards that finished, with a note. When no board finished in time it throws TimeoutException, saying what the first board was still doing.
List<TumbleData> GenerateBatch(TumbleSettings settings, int count, CancellationToken cancel = default) A pack of verified puzzles with seeds root-0, root-1 and so on.
Task<TumbleData> GenerateAsync(...), GenerateVerifiedAsync(...)The same on a background thread.
Task<List<TumbleData>> GenerateBatchAsync(TumbleSettings settings, int count, IProgress<int> progress = null, CancellationToken cancel = default) A batch on a background thread, reporting how many are done (1 to count).
bool IsSound(TumbleData m, TumbleSettings s)The hard requirements verified generation uses: solvable, the par inside the window, the coverage, and no way to get stuck when asked.
int Shortfall(TumbleData m, TumbleSettings s)How many of the targets, keys, switches, avoid, one-way, multi-face, turntable and crumbling tiles and portal pairs asked for the puzzle is missing. 0 means every one is there.

TumbleProgress holds Attempt and Attempts (the board being tried, from 1, and how many may be tried), Stage in plain words ("placing keys and doors", "solving and rating"), Seconds since the puzzle started and Limit. Its ToString() reads "board 2 of 40: solving and rating (1.3 s)".

TumbleSettings holds every input to the generator. Each field, its range and what it does is on the Generating page. It also has:

TumbleSettings Clamp()Puts every field into its valid range, in place, and returns the same object.
TumbleSettings Clone()A copy. The custom outline's rows are copied too, so the two never share them.
EnumValues

TumbleData

One puzzle, plain and serializable. The generator fills in the board and the verdict, and you can build one by hand too (see A puzzle by hand).

MemberWhat it is
int Width, HeightThe board's size in cells.
List<int> Walls, Holes, OutsideRaised blocks, gaps in the floor, and cells the outline cut away. The cube can't roll onto any of them. BoardOutline Outline is the outline the board was cut to.
TumbleMode ModeReachGoal or StampAll.
int Start, int StartOrientationThe cube's starting cell and how it sits there.
int Goal, int GoalFaceThe goal cell (-1 in StampAll), and the face that has to be down on it, or pressed on every target.
List<int> Targets, bool OrderedStampAll's target tiles, 1 to 8 of them, and whether they have to be stamped in list order.
List<AvoidTile> AvoidTiles with a Face that must never be down on them.
List<OneWayTile> OneWaysTiles crossed only in their arrow's Dir.
List<int> Keys, List<int> KeyFaces, List<DoorTile> DoorsKeys[k] is the tile holding key k. KeyFaces is empty, or holds one face per key that has to land on it (-1 for any face). Each door names the Key that opens it.
List<SwitchTile> Switches, List<GateTile> GatesOne switch per Channel, with a Face (-1 for any). Each gate has a Channel and StartsOpen, and every press of its switch flips it.
List<MultiFaceTile> MultiFacesTiles that need 1 to 3 faces pressed on them before the puzzle can be won. Faces is a mask (bit f is face f), and Needs(face) reads it.
List<TurntableTile> TurntablesTiles that turn the cube a quarter turn in place, Clockwise or not, every time it rolls onto them.
List<int> CrumblingTiles that break once the cube rolls off them.
List<PortalPair> PortalsPairs of tiles A and B. Rolling onto one, the cube comes out of the other and rolls once more the same way.
string Seed, TumbleSettings SettingsWhat it was made from, so it can be made again. Both are empty on a hand-made puzzle.
bool Solvable, int ParWhether it can be won, and the proven fewest rolls (-1 when unsolvable or not verified).
long OptimalCount, bool CountComplete, bool UniqueHow many best solutions there are, whether that count is complete (it stops at 1,000,000,000), and whether there is exactly one.
bool CanGetStuckWhether a player can reach a position that can never be won.
int Walk, int DetourThe fewest rolls ignoring the faces (start to goal, or over every target in the best order), and Par - Walk: the rolls spent turning the cube.
int DifficultyThe 1 to 5 star rating (0 when unsolvable or not proven).
int TurningSpots, float Coverage, bool TrimmedOpen 2 x 2 squares where the cube can turn on the spot, the share of the tiles the best solution rolls over (0 to 1), and whether the board was trimmed to the puzzle.
List<int> Solution, List<int> MovesOne best solution: the cells it passes, start first, and its directions.
List<string> NotesPlain-words notes from the generator and the solver. The Generating page lists them.
CellCount, CellAt(x, y), X(cell), Y(cell), Neighbor(cell, dir)Cell arithmetic. CellAt and Neighbor give -1 off the board.
IsWall, IsHole, IsOutside, IsFloor(cell)What a cell is. A floor cell is on the board, inside the outline, and not a wall or a hole.
MaxTargets, MaxKeys, MaxSwitches, MaxMultiFaces, MaxTurntables, MaxCrumbling, MaxPortals, MaxBitsThe limits: 8 targets, 4 keys, 4 switches, 4 multi-face tiles, 4 turntables, 4 crumbling tiles and 2 portal pairs. Targets, keys, switches, multi-face faces and crumbling tiles count 10 at most together, because each one doubles the positions the solver weighs.

Each tile type is a small struct with a constructor and a short ToString(): new AvoidTile(cell, face), new OneWayTile(cell, dir), new DoorTile(cell, key), new SwitchTile(cell, channel, face = -1), new GateTile(cell, channel, startsOpen = false), new MultiFaceTile(cell, facesMask), new TurntableTile(cell, clockwise) and new PortalPair(a, b).

Cube

The rolling law, built once into tables, so the solver, the generator and the play rules all turn the cube the same way.

MemberWhat it does
Faces, Orientations, Directions6, 24 and 4. North, East, South and West are 0 to 3.
int StandardFace 0 on top and face 4 facing north, so face 5 is down.
int Make(int top, int north)The orientation with these faces on top and facing north, or -1 when the two are the same face or opposite.
int Top(o), int Bottom(o), int FaceOn(o, side)The face on top, the face down, and the face on any CubeSide.
CubeSide SideOf(o, face)Which side a face is on.
int Rolled(o, dir)The orientation after one roll that way.
int Spun(o, clockwise)The orientation after a turntable's quarter turn, seen from above.
Opposite(face), OppositeDirection(dir), Axis(side)The opposite face, the opposite direction, and the way a side faces (x east, y north, z up).

Playing: TumbleRules

The rules a game plays by. They are the same rules the solver counted with, so a game built on them always agrees with the par.

MethodWhat it does
TumblePlay NewGame(TumbleData m)The starting position. In StampAll a target the cube starts on with the goal face down is stamped already.
bool TryRoll(TumbleData m, TumblePlay play, int dir, out string reason)Rolls the cube one cell if the rules allow it and updates the position in place: landing on a key takes it, landing on a switch flips its gates, landing on a target with the goal face down stamps it, and rolling off a crumbling tile breaks it. On a refusal it returns false with a short reason, such as "the door at 3 needs key 0". An overload leaves out the reason.
RollBlock Blocked(TumbleData m, TumblePlay play, int dir)Why a roll isn't allowed, without changing anything.
bool Hint(TumbleData m, TumblePlay play, out int dir, int stateBudget = TumbleSolver.DefaultBudget) The first roll of a best way on from here. False when the puzzle is won or can't be won from here. The budget is never less than every position the board can hold, so a hint is always exact.
bool IsSolved(TumbleData m, TumblePlay play)True once the puzzle is won. Every multi-face tile also has to have all its faces pressed.
bool IsGateOpen(m, play, cell), bool IsPressed(m, play, tile, face), bool IsCrumbled(m, play, tile)How a gate, a multi-face tile's face, or a crumbling tile stands in this position. tile is an index into MultiFaces or Crumbling.
int NextTarget(TumbleData m, TumblePlay play)Ordered StampAll: the index of the target to stamp next, or -1 once all are stamped. Otherwise always -1.
bool Validate(TumbleData m, out string error)Checks that the data makes sense: cells on the board, nothing sharing a tile it can't share, the limits, a real orientation and goal face, and not won at the start. It doesn't say whether the puzzle can be solved; the solver does.

TumblePlay is the position in play, and the only thing a game has to keep between rolls:

MemberWhat it is
int Cell, int OrientationWhere the cube is and how it sits.
int Stamped, bool IsStamped(index)The targets stamped, bit i for Targets[i].
int Keys, bool HasKey(key)The keys taken. A key's doors stay open for good.
int Flips, bool IsFlipped(channel)The switches pressed an odd number of times.
int PressedThe multi-face presses so far. Read them with TumbleRules.IsPressed.
int WornThe crumbling tiles the cube has stood on. Read them with TumbleRules.IsCrumbled, because the one under the cube hasn't broken yet.
TumblePlay Clone()A copy, for undo.

Solving and rating

MemberWhat it does
TumbleVerdict TumbleSolver.Solve(TumbleData m, int stateBudget = DefaultBudget)Solves a puzzle from its start.
TumbleVerdict TumbleSolver.SolveFrom(TumbleData m, TumblePlay play, int stateBudget = DefaultBudget) Solves on from a position in play: its Par is the rolls still needed.
TumbleVerdict TumbleSolver.Verify(TumbleData m, int stateBudget = DefaultBudget)Solves and writes the verdict, the walk, the stars, the turning spots and the coverage into the puzzle. Use it after building or changing a puzzle by hand.
int TumbleSolver.StatesOnBoard(TumbleData m)Every position the puzzle can have: floor tiles x 12 (x 24 with a turntable or a portal) x 2 for each target, key, switch, multi-face face and crumbling tile. Pass it as the budget and the search is never cut.
TumbleSolver.DefaultBudget, TumbleSolver.CountCap500,000 positions, and the 1,000,000,000 where the best-solution count stops.
int PlainWalk(m), int WalkDistance(m, from, to)The walk the stars measure the detour against, and the fewest rolls between two cells, both ignoring the faces (walls, holes, the edge and one-way tiles still count).
int[] ParFromEveryState(m), bool[] CanGetStuckFromEveryState(m)Whole-board tables, indexed cell * 24 + orientation: the exact par from every position as a fresh start (-1 when it can't win), and whether the cube can get stuck from there. This is how the generator picks a start.
bool[] BestSolutionCells(m), bool[] ShortestPlainWalkCells(m), int[] PlainWalkFromEveryCell(m), WalkFromEveryCell(m, to), WalkToEveryCell(m, from)Which cells lie on some best solution or some shortest plain walk, and walking distances over the whole board. Trimming uses them.
TumbleDifficulty.Stars(m), Score(m), StarsFor(score, mode, targets) The stars from a solved puzzle: the score is par + 2 x detour, and the bands it is read against (Bands, StampBands, BandsFor(mode, targets)) are on the Generating page.

TumbleVerdict holds Solvable, Par, Count, CountComplete, Unique, CanGetStuck, BudgetCut, Explored, and one best solution as Path (cells, the cube's first), Orientations (how it sits at each) and Moves (directions).

The generator, Hint and the inspectors' Verify never use a budget smaller than StatesOnBoard, so their results are always exact. Only a solve you call yourself with a smaller budget can be cut short, and then the puzzle's notes say so.

A puzzle by hand

Fill in the board and the puzzle, check it with TumbleRules.Validate, then let the solver write the verdict. This 5 x 4 board puts the key in the top left corner and a door on both ways into the goal, so the cube has to fetch the key first. The comments show what it prints.

using System.Collections.Generic;
using System.Linq;
using SimpleTumble;
using UnityEngine;

var m = new TumbleData
{
    Width = 5, Height = 4,
    Mode = TumbleMode.ReachGoal,
    Start = 0,                          // cell (0, 0), bottom left
    StartOrientation = Cube.Standard,   // face 0 on top, face 5 down
    Goal = 4,                           // cell (4, 0), bottom right
    GoalFace = 0,                       // win with face 0 down
    Walls = new List<int> { 7, 12 },
    Keys = new List<int> { 15 },        // key 0 on cell (0, 3), top left
    Doors = new List<DoorTile> { new DoorTile(3, 0), new DoorTile(9, 0) },   // both ways into the goal
};

if (!TumbleRules.Validate(m, out string error)) Debug.LogError(error);
TumbleSolver.Verify(m, TumbleSolver.StatesOnBoard(m));   // a budget this big never cuts the search
Debug.Log(TumbleAudit.Short(m));                          // 5 x 4, reach the goal, par 10, 1 star, unique
Debug.Log(string.Join(" ", m.Moves.Select(d => "NESW"[d])));   // N N N S E S S E E E

Without the key and its doors the same board takes 8 rolls, so the key really is part of the puzzle. Rolling east from the start runs into the shut door on the third roll, and TryRoll refuses it with "the door at 3 needs key 0".

Presets, Auto and audit

MemberWhat it does
TumblePresets.AllThe 20 presets. Each TumblePreset has a Name ("Difficulty/Big board, trimmed"), a Description, and Make(), which returns fresh settings.
TumbleSettings TumblePresets.Tune(TumbleSettings s, List<string> changes)The Auto button: a copy with settings that fight each other fixed, and a line in changes for each fix.
int TumblePresets.Bits(TumbleSettings s)How many of the 10 bits a request uses: targets (StampAll), keys, switches, multi-face faces and crumbling tiles.
bool TumbleAudit.MeetsRequest(TumbleData m)True when a puzzle got everything its settings asked for. A hand-made puzzle only needs to be solvable.
List<string> TumbleAudit.Missing(TumbleData m)What it missed, in plain words, such as "2 of 3 keys" or "4 stars, asked 2". Empty when it got everything.
bool TumbleAudit.Verify(TumbleData m, out string before, out string after)Solves again and restamps the verdict. Returns true when the verdict changed. Data that fails Validate is left alone, with the reason in after.
TumbleData TumbleAudit.Reroll(TumbleData m)A fresh puzzle from the same settings and a new seed, or null for a hand-made one. An overload takes a cancel, progress and a time limit.
bool TumbleAudit.Generated(TumbleData m)True when the puzzle came from the generator (it has a seed and settings).
string TumbleAudit.Short(TumbleData m)One line, such as "7 x 7, reach the goal, par 18, 3 stars".

Pictures

TumbleExport draws a puzzle with no Unity code at all, so it also works in tools and on a server.

MemberWhat it does
byte[] Png(TumbleData m, ExportOptions options = null)A .png file's bytes.
string Svg(TumbleData m, ExportOptions options = null)An SVG document, which stays sharp at any size.
byte[] Pixels(TumbleData m, ExportOptions options, out int width, out int height)Raw RGBA bytes, top row first. Another overload also gives a PictureFrame, whose CellAt(px, py) and CellCentre(cell, out px, out py) map a point on the picture to a cell and back.
int FitSize(TumbleData m, ExportOptions options, int maxWidth, int maxHeight)The Size that fits a picture in a box.
TumblePlay PlayAt(TumbleData m, int step)The position after the first step rolls of the stored solution.
List<LegendIcon> Legend(TumbleData m, int step, int iconPx)The picture's legend as lines of text, each with its icon, for drawing beside the board in your own font.
NetPixels(orientation, cell, ...), NetSize, NetBoxThe cube unfolded, with the top ringed and the face down boxed apart, as the editor's cube panel shows it.
FaceAtlasPixels(cell, ...), ChipAtlasPixels(cell, ...)The six face chips side by side, for texturing a 3D cube or marking a face on a tile.

ExportOptions has Style, Size (the board's longer side in pixels, 1600 by default), Step (-1 for the start, or a step of the solution, with the rolls so far drawn as a line), Legend and Cube (false leaves the cube off, for a 3D view that puts a real one on top). TumbleLook has the six faces' Colours, ColourNames and ShapeNames, Look(face) ("green diamond"), Name(face) ("the green diamond (face 3)") and IsLight(face), which says whether to draw the face's shape dark on it.

TypeWhat it does
TumbleSketchA puzzle as drawing marks in board units, which is what the pictures and the editor preview draw. Build(m, play, rolls) gives one Mark per thing on the board (a Kind, its cell, numbers and whether it is done in that position) and the solution as Lines. Describe(m, cell, play) is a tooltip for one cell, and Frames(m) is the stored solution roll by roll, each with a caption.
TumbleView3DThe numbers behind a 3D view, with no Unity types: CubeMesh(orientation, ...) builds a cube whose sides carry the right faces, Rest and Rolling(m, cell, orientation, dir, t) give the cube's Pose at rest and partway through a roll (portals and turntables included), and BoardUV lays a board picture on a 3D slab.

ScriptableObjects

TypeWhat it holds
TumblePuzzleAssetOne puzzle: Puzzle and Label, plus shortcuts to its verdict (Width, Height, Mode, Solvable, IsUnique, OptimalCount, CanGetStuck, Par, Difficulty, Seed) and Set(data, label).
TumblePuzzlePackAn ordered list of puzzles: PackName, Puzzles, Count, Get(index) (null when out of range), Set, Add, Replace(index, data) and Clear().
PuzzleAssetsCreatePuzzle(data, label) and CreatePack(puzzles, packName) make new instances in memory, which you can save with AssetDatabase.CreateAsset in the editor or use at runtime.
IPuzzleProviderBoth asset types implement it: AppendTo(List<TumbleData> levels) adds their puzzles in order, so one list in the inspector can take either.

Both are created by the Tumble Generator's Save menu, not by an empty right-click Create.

Demo components

These are in SimpleTumble.Demo. The Demos page explains how they fit together.

TypeWhat it does
TumbleDemoThe one component the demo scene needs. It builds the board, game, HUD, hub, sounds and effects in code and runs the flow. Fields: Camera, UiThroughCamera, Pitch, HdrpLook and a level pack per mode. Methods include Setup(), PlayEndless(mode, shaped, stars), PlayLevel(mode, index), Generate(mode, settings), Begin(puzzle), NextGame() and BackToHub().
TumbleGamePlays a puzzle on a TumbleBoardView: rolls against par, undo, restart, hints and solving by itself, with keyboard, swipe, tap and camera controls (KeyboardInput, PointerInput and CameraControls turn each off or on). Start a game with Begin(puzzle), drive it with Roll(dir), Push(screenDir), Tap(screenPoint), Undo(), Restart(), ShowHint() and StartAutoSolve(), and listen to Moved, Won, Message, Cue and Moment. Why(m, play, dir, block) words a refusal for a player.
TumbleBoardViewBuilds a puzzle in 3D from meshes made in code, with no prefabs: Build(puzzle), Show(play) (every gimmick as it stands in that position), Place(pose), FrameCamera(camera), CellWorld(cell) and LocalBounds.
TumbleHudThe in-game HUD and win panel, in uGUI with the built-in font. FreeAreas(camera) says where the board can go without the HUD covering it.
TumbleHubThe front screen: the welcome panel, the mode cards, and each mode's Endless and Level Pack setup.
TumbleDemoMode, TumbleModes, TumbleEndlessThe four demo modes, their names, colours and sample puzzles, and the Endless recipe for each mode, board and star count.
TumblePackProgressEach pack level's best rolls, kept in PlayerPrefs: Best, Record, Clear and NameOf.
TumbleBeeperSounds made in code for every TumbleCue, with no audio files.
TumbleEffectsSmall effects (dust, bursts, rings and confetti) from a pool of primitives, for every TumbleMoment.
TumbleMeshes, MeshBuilderThe meshes, made in code: the cube for each orientation, its face atlas, and the tiles and gimmicks.
DemoLook, DemoHdrpThe demo's colours and materials for the active render pipeline, and the HDRP volume that gives it the same look.

Building blocks

The pieces the generator is made of are public too, for anyone who wants to build their own pipeline. Most projects never need them.

TypeWhat it does
TumbleBuilderBuild(settings, seed) makes one puzzle from clamped settings and an exact seed and solves it, which is what every TumbleGenerator method calls.
TumbleShapeBoard outlines: Cut(w, h, outline, notes), TryParse(rows, ...) for a custom outline, KeepLargest, and TrimToPuzzle(m, margin, mode), which cuts away tiles far from the solution without changing the par.
TumbleLayout, ObstaclePlacer, ObstacleRules, ObstacleMetricsPlacing walls and holes so the free tiles stay connected and evenly spread, and counting TurningSpots.
RngThe small deterministic random generator, seeded from a string.
